# B2B SaaS Case Study Patterns

B2B/SaaS product design has unique characteristics that should shape how case studies are written.
Read this file when the user is writing a case study for a B2B or SaaS product feature.

## What Makes B2B Case Studies Different

### Users vs. Buyers
In B2B, the person using the product is often not the person who bought it. Case studies should acknowledge both audiences:
- Show empathy for the end user (the operations manager, the analyst, the engineer)
- Demonstrate understanding of buyer concerns (ROI, adoption rates, time-to-value)

### Complexity is a Feature, Not a Bug
B2B products often handle genuinely complex workflows. Don't simplify your case study to the point where it sounds like a consumer app redesign. Embrace the complexity — show that you can navigate intricate domains and make them usable.

### Enterprise Constraints Are Real
Mention constraints that show you understand enterprise product design:
- Legacy system integration
- Backward compatibility requirements
- Security and compliance considerations
- Multi-tenant architecture implications on design
- Admin vs. end-user experience considerations
- Progressive rollout strategies (feature flags, beta groups)

## Metrics That Matter in B2B

### Adoption & Engagement
- Daily/weekly active users
- Feature adoption rate (% of eligible users who activated the feature)
- Session frequency and duration
- "Stickiness" (DAU/MAU ratio)

### Efficiency & Performance
- Task completion time (before/after)
- Error rates
- Support ticket volume related to the feature
- Steps to complete a workflow (before/after)

### Business Impact
- Impact on customer retention/churn
- Influence on deal close rates (if the feature is demo'd in sales)
- Reduction in onboarding time
- Customer expansion (upsell/cross-sell influenced by the feature)
- NPS or CSAT changes

### When You Don't Have Exact Numbers
Use these patterns to show impact directionally:
- "Adopted by the majority of enterprise accounts within the first quarter"
- "Cited in multiple customer renewal conversations as a key reason to stay"
- "Reduced the #1 support ticket category by a significant margin"
- "Became the default workflow for the platform's largest customer segment"

## Common B2B Project Types & How to Frame Them

### Dashboard / Analytics Feature
Frame around: Decision-making speed, data accessibility, self-serve vs. dependency on analysts
Senior signal: Show that you understood what decisions users needed to make, not just what data to display

### Workflow / Process Tool
Frame around: Time savings, error reduction, process standardization
Senior signal: Show how you mapped the existing workflow, identified bottlenecks, and simplified without losing critical steps

### Admin / Settings / Permissions
Frame around: Security confidence, self-serve administration, reduced IT dependency
Senior signal: These are "unglamorous" projects that senior designers tackle. Frame it as infrastructure work that enables scale.

### Onboarding / Activation
Frame around: Time-to-value, activation rates, support burden reduction
Senior signal: Show that you defined what "activated" means and worked with data to validate it

### Integration / API Experience
Frame around: Developer experience, integration success rates, ecosystem growth
Senior signal: Show that you can design for technical users and care about developer ergonomics

## Describing Your Role on B2B Projects

B2B projects are almost always team efforts. Be precise about your contribution:

**Good ownership language:**
- "I was the sole designer on a team of 6 engineers and 1 PM"
- "I led the design workstream within a larger platform initiative"
- "I drove the discovery and research phase, then partnered with a visual designer for the final UI"
- "I owned the end-to-end experience from research through post-launch iteration"

**Red flags to avoid:**
- "We designed..." without clarifying your specific contribution
- "The team decided..." — who on the team? Did you influence the decision?
- Taking credit for work you facilitated vs. executed (be honest, it reads better)
